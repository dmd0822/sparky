Options

1) Option A — Centralized e-stop + hardware cutoff (Recommended — safer)
- Summary: Use a single, physically accessible emergency-stop (e-stop) system that cuts power at a central relay and an additional local mute/cutoff at device-level.
- Pros:
  - Simple, auditable single control point for safety officers.
  - Easier to test and certify prior to HIL.
  - Minimizes risk of partial shutdown states.
- Cons:
  - Requires a central relay and wiring changes to test rig.
  - Slightly higher initial hardware cost and integration time.

2) Option B — Distributed device-level cutoffs
- Summary: Equip each subsystem with an independent cutoff/mute switch and rely on software interlocks to coordinate shutoff.
- Pros:
  - Lower initial wiring complexity; modular per-subsystem implementation.
  - Localized control can allow partial shutdowns for reduced-risk debugging.
- Cons:
  - Higher risk of inconsistent states during emergency; depends on software correctness.
  - More complex verification and test matrix.

(If more options are required, add here — e.g., hybrid approaches, redundant e-stops.)

Recommendation
- Choose Option A (Centralized e-stop + hardware cutoff). It is the safer, more auditable approach and aligns with HIL safety acceptance criteria. The extra hardware/integration cost is justified by reduced risk and easier certification.
