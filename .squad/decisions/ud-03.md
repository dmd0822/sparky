Options

1) Option A — Centralized e-stop + hardware cutoff
- Summary: Use a single, physically accessible emergency-stop (e-stop) system that cuts power at a central relay and an additional local mute/cutoff at device-level.
- Pros:
  - Simple, auditable single control point for safety officers.
  - Easier to test and certify prior to HIL.
  - Minimizes risk of partial shutdown states.
- Cons:
  - Requires a central relay and wiring changes to test rig.
  - Slightly higher initial hardware cost and integration time.

2) Option B — Distributed device-level cutoffs
- Summary: Equip each subsystem with an independent cutoff/mute switch and rely on software interlocks to coordinate shutoff.
- Pros:
  - Lower initial wiring complexity; modular per-subsystem implementation.
  - Localized control can allow partial shutdowns for reduced-risk debugging.
- Cons:
  - Higher risk of inconsistent states during emergency; depends on software correctness.
  - More complex verification and test matrix.

3) Option C — Hybrid approach (Recommended)
- Summary: Combine a centralized e-stop that cuts main power with local device-level cutoffs/mute switches. The central e-stop provides a guaranteed full-power cutoff; local cutoffs allow safe, rapid local isolation for maintenance and partial troubleshooting.
- Pros:
  - Retains a single auditable full-power cutoff while enabling modular local control for debugging.
  - Reduces risk compared to purely distributed approach while avoiding single-point operational limitations.
  - Simplifies certification of the full-stop path while preserving flexibility.
- Cons:
  - Requires both central relay hardware and per-device cutoff hardware — higher component count and integration work.
  - Needs clear SOPs to avoid operator confusion about which control to use.

Recommendation
- Choose the Hybrid approach (Option C). This combines the safety and auditability of a central e-stop with the operational flexibility of device-level cutoffs. It balances risk reduction, certification simplicity for the full-stop path, and practical on-bench debugging capability. Proceed with central relay procurement plus staged installation of local cutoffs, starting with the highest-risk subsystems.
